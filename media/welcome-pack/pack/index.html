How they were made: Toran's real screens were captured from a copy of the site filled with the example mandir, then animated with a pointer, zooms and typing in Toran's own fonts and colours. The music is original and synthesised, so there's nothing to license. Every event, person and number in them is made up, and each film says so in the corner. To add a spoken voice (in English, Gujarati or Hindi), record the voiceover scripts below; they're timed to the films. The source is in the media/welcome-pack folder of the media repository, so the films can be re-made when the screens change.

Signing up a mandir

From first message to a committee that runs its own page

About two weeks per mandir, and around two hours of your time. Do it in person where you can: committees decide on trust, after aarti on a Friday or Sunday morning.

  1. Day 0

    Find the right person

    The secretary, a trustee or whoever posts the programme. Use the mandir's own website, its noticeboard, or a visit. Don't collect names or numbers from social media.

  2. Day 0

    Send the first message and the mandirs film

    "First message to a mandir" below. Show their page on your phone if you meet in person: it already has their address, opening times and how to get there.

  3. Day 1 to 3

    A 15-minute conversation

    Listen first: how do they share the programme now, what went wrong last Navratri or Diwali, who decides on new things. Then show adding a poster on your phone. Leave the one-page guide (toran.org.uk/for-mandirs/guide/print).

  4. Same day

    They ask to look after the page

    Either from "Look after this page" on the mandir's page, or send them the join link and QR code from /ops/mandirs → the mandir → "Join link for its committee".

  5. Within a day

    Check, then approve

    Ring the mandir office or another committee member you trust to confirm this person helps run it. Approve in /ops. Then send "After approving" with the committee film.

  6. First week

    Add their first events together

    Ask for their next poster. If they're busy, add it yourself through the review queue so their page looks alive straight away; they publish it with one tap.

  7. Day 7

    The weekly habit

    Show them "Send this week's events" and agree a day, often Thursday, for sending it to their groups. Ask them to invite one colleague, so the page doesn't depend on one person.

  8. Day 14

    Show it working, then hand over to families

    Open Numbers with them. Give them the families film and the noticeboard poster with a QR code (from their page's print kit), and the families message below for their groups.

Weekly nudge to committees

Anything new for this weekend? Add the poster on your Toran page, then tap "Send this week's events" for your groups. About five minutes. 🙏

"Is it really free?"

Yes, for mandirs and families. There are no adverts, and Toran doesn't sell or share anyone's data. Families don't need an account at all; committee members give only their email address.

For committee members

Toran in five steps

  1. Log in at toran.org.uk with your email. Tap the link Toran sends you.
  2. Open your mandir from Your account.
  3. Add events: a photo of the poster, a pasted WhatsApp message, typed in, or ticked festivals.
  4. Check each one, then tap Publish.
  5. Every week, tap Send this week's events for your groups.

Seva: open Seva on your page, add roles and shifts, and send the rota to your volunteers.

For families

Find what's on

  1. Open toran.org.uk on your phone.
  2. Tap Find near me to see events nearest first.
  3. Open an event to add it to your calendar or send it on WhatsApp.
  4. Tap Follow on your mandirs; they gather in My Toran.
  5. Keep Toran on your home screen: Share, then Add to Home Screen.

Free, no account, and nothing about you is tracked.

Questions you'll be asked

Short, true answers

Will this replace our WhatsApp group?

No. It makes the group work better: one link to everything coming up, and a weekly message Toran writes for you. Families who aren't in the group can still find you.

Who can change our page?

Only committee members Toran has approved, after checking with the mandir. Each of them can invite colleagues, and anyone can be removed. Toran's admins can correct a mistake if someone reports one.

What if Toran reads our poster wrongly?

Nothing it reads goes live on its own. Every event waits, marked "read by machine: check every detail", until someone on the committee checks it and publishes it.

What do you keep about people?

For committee members: their email address and which mandir they help run. For families: nothing. What they follow, their plan and their seva slots are kept on their own phone. Visits are counted as totals, without cookies.

Our elders don't use computers.

Toran works on any phone, and one younger member can keep the page up to date for everyone. Families only need to open a link. Until a committee is ready, you can add their posters for them.

Is it in Gujarati and Hindi?

Yes. The site works in English, Gujarati and Hindi, and events can have their name and description in Gujarati. The Gujarati wording is still being checked by a reviewer, and the site says so.

Do volunteers have to sign up for seva?

No. They take a slot in one tap, with no account, and Toran doesn't ask their name. The committee sees how many are coming. Roles that need a DBS check are marked, and the mandir checks those itself; Toran never sees certificates.

We don't want to be listed.

That's fine. Toran hides the mandir's page on request, from "Correct or remove something" at the foot of every page.

We already have a website.

Keep it. Toran links to it, can read your events from your website's calendar, and gives you a calendar link to show Toran's events on your own site.

Who is behind Toran?

Milan, who is building it to make mandir events easier to find. [Add the company name and number once it's registered.]

Sharing to Toran doesn't work on my iPhone.

iPhones don't let websites appear in the Share menu. Copy the message in WhatsApp, then paste it into "Paste a WhatsApp message" on your committee page.

When things go wrong

What to do, quickly

The site is down

Check toran.org.uk/api/health and Vercel's status page. Sentry emails you errors, and the Uptime check on GitHub opens an issue. The last good deployment can be promoted again in Vercel → Deployments in one click.

Someone asks what Toran keeps about them, or to delete it

A legal request: reply within one month. Committee members can leave from their page; for anything else, Claude can find and remove it. Note the date you replied.

An event is wrong

The committee edits it from Next up or Coming up. Reports from "Something wrong here? Tell us" arrive in /ops → Needs attention; fix it or ask the committee, ideally within two working days.

Two people ask to run the same mandir

Don't approve either until you've rung the mandir office. Several people can look after one page, so both may be right.

A poster was read wrongly

Nothing went live, so fix it with "Fix something first" before publishing, or Discard and type it in. Tell Claude if a kind of poster keeps going wrong.

The nightly job didn't run

/ops → Needs attention warns after a day and a half. Vercel → Settings → Cron Jobs → Run starts it by hand.

A mandir wants to leave Toran

Hide its page in /ops → Mandirs (Shown to the public? → Hidden), and remove committee access. Thank them and ask why: it's useful to know.

A safeguarding worry about seva

The mandir runs its own volunteers. Pass the concern to the mandir's committee straight away, and turn off online sign-up for that event if needed.

Voiceover scripts

Words to record over each film

Each line starts at the time shown, and says what's on screen. Read slowly and warmly; a Gujarati reading by a volunteer will mean a lot to elders. Record each film in one take on a phone in a quiet room, and Claude can add it to the film.
